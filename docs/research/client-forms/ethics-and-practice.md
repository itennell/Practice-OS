Template wording, about 8th-grade level (one line per element):
- purpose_of_services: We will talk about what our work together is for and what to expect.
- risks: Therapy can help, but talking about hard things can sometimes feel uncomfortable at first.
- payer_limits: If you use insurance, your plan may limit how many sessions it pays for or what it covers.
- costs_fees: This form lists my fees, how billing works and what happens if a bill goes unpaid.
- alternatives: You can ask me about other kinds of help, and I will tell you about them.
- right_to_refuse: You can say no to any service or stop at any time, and I will explain what that could mean.
- consent_timeframe: This consent lasts until we stop working together or you take it back in writing.
- questions: You can ask questions now or at any time, and I will answer them.
- plain_language: If anything here is unclear, tell me and I will explain it another way.
- interpreter: If you would like an interpreter, I will help arrange one.
- nature_course: I will explain how sessions usually go and about how long this kind of work may take.
- third_parties: If anyone else is involved, such as a payer, a referring person or a family member, I will tell you who they are and what they may learn.
- clinician_qualifications: Here are my license, training, experience and how I usually work.
- incapacitation_plan: If I suddenly cannot work, a named colleague will contact you to help you continue care and get your records.
- technology_role: This form explains how I use phone, email, text, video and online tools in my practice.
- diagnosis_use: If I give a diagnosis or use a test, I will explain what it means and who will see it.
- confidentiality_limits: What you say stays private, except for a few cases the law requires or allows, which are listed here.
- imminent_harm: If I believe someone is in serious danger, I may need to share information to keep them safe.
- supervision_disclosure: I talk with a supervisor or consultant about my work. [If in training: I am in training and my supervisor is (name).]
- records_info: I keep records of our work, and you can ask to see or get a copy of them.
- recording_consent: I will never record or let anyone watch a session without your written permission.
- electronic_search: I will not look you up online without your permission, except in an emergency.
- mandated_services: If a court or agency sent you here, I will tell you what I must report and to whom before we begin.
- documented: We will both sign this form, and you will get a copy.
- ongoing: We can come back to this form at any time, and I will tell you if anything changes.
- capacity_free_consent: Sign only if you understand this form and are choosing therapy freely.
- client_decides: Big decisions about your relationships are yours to make, not mine.

Template wording:
- who_is_client: In couples or family work, the client is [the couple / the family / each of you], and I will treat each of you fairly.
- default_unit_client: Unless we agree in writing on something else, the couple or family as a whole is my client.
- confidentiality_agreement: Together we will agree in writing on what stays private between each of you and me.
- no_guarantee: I keep things private, but I cannot promise that others in the room will.
- secrets_policy: [Pick one] I do not keep secrets between partners. / I will not share what one of you tells me alone without that person's written OK.
- release_outside: I will share information outside therapy only if each adult in therapy signs a written OK, unless the law requires it.
- records_access_each: Because the record belongs to all of you, each adult must sign before I release it.
- conflicting_roles_divorce: If you separate or divorce, I will not take one person's side or act as a witness for one of you.
- no_custody_eval: I will not do custody or visitation evaluations for anyone I see in therapy.
- individual_sessions: If I meet with one of you alone, that meeting is part of the couples or family work.
- court_testimony: If our records are subpoenaed, I will claim privilege for all of you. I will testify only if the law requires it or every adult agrees in writing.
- who_decides_relationships: Decisions about staying together, separating or marrying are yours, not mine.

## Open questions / disputed

1. AAMFT version: aamft.org is behind a login and blocked the scraper. A search snippet says the live site words 6.1 differently and has a new "6.3 Technology and Consent to Treat" requiring written telehealth risk disclosure. That suggests a revision after 2015 that I could not verify. Our citations use the 2015 text from a state mirror. Someone needs to check the current AAMFT code by hand; standard numbers may have shifted.
2. ACA revision: the 2014 code is still in force as of the ACA ethics page (public comment expected early 2026; adoption date unknown). Recheck before launch, because standard IDs (A.2.b, H.2.a) may change.
3. APA telepsychology 2024: the final text could not be fetched (page empty, PDF blocked). The guideline numbering (consent = Guideline 3 in 2013, possibly Guideline 2 in 2024) and its wording come from search snippets only. Guidelines are aspirational, not enforceable.
4. NASW Technology Standards wording comes from a university mirror PDF, not the socialworkers.org PDF, which returned 404.
5. HHS and 45 CFR 164.524 quotes come from search snippets. The IL-law agent should confirm, along with IL rules on fees, timelines and minors' access (the IL Mental Health and Developmental Disabilities Confidentiality Act may be stricter than HIPAA).
6. Couples secrets policy: the codes conflict in their defaults. AAMFT 2.2 bars sharing one person's confidences without written permission. A "no-secrets" policy therefore needs each partner's written waiver up front. ACA defaults to the unit as the client. Recommend the form offers a clinician-chosen option, and each adult signs.
7. Ending services for nonpayment: NASW 1.17(c) sets conditions (fee terms clear in advance, no imminent danger, consequences discussed). ACA A.11.c simply permits it. APA has no direct rule. Recommend applying NASW's conditions for all licenses (strictest).
8. Medicare no-show fee: CMS allows it only if the same policy and amount apply to everyone, and it is never billed to Medicare. Secondary sources say Medicaid bars it and some payer contracts forbid it. Medicaid was not verified here.
9. Sliding scale: no primary ethics source requires a sliding scale or specific criteria. All the detail is practice-management guidance (vendor blogs), not The Trust or APA Services; their pages were not found. The vendor advice to keep "proof of income" conflicts with our no-financial-storage stance. Recommend self-report on a blank form only.
10. The Trust and CPH/HPSO risk articles were not reached (no search hits; likely members-only), so the best-practice rows rely on code text plus APA's checklist.
11. Not verified: whether IL rules for LCSW, LCPC and LMFT incorporate these codes by reference.

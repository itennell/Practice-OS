# Client forms research: Illinois rules (checked 2026-10-29)

Research for Practice-OS client forms. Primary text only: ilga.gov (ILCS and the JCAR Illinois Administrative Code). All pages pulled 2026-10-29 through self-hosted Firecrawl. This is not legal advice. The clinician confirms.

**Key**
- `level`: `required` means an Illinois statute or board rule says it (for the listed license types). `best_practice` means it is not an Illinois requirement but is advisable, or Illinois is silent and the national ethics code adopted by the board covers it.
- `applies_to`: LCSW (Clinical Social Work and Social Work Practice Act, 225 ILCS 20; 68 IAC 1470), LCPC/LPC (225 ILCS 107; 68 IAC 1375), LMFT (225 ILCS 55; 68 IAC 1283), Psych (clinical psychologist, 225 ILCS 15; 68 IAC 1400). ALL means all four.
- Every quote is copied word for word from the source and is 25 words or fewer. Template sentences are written at about an 8th-grade reading level.

**Source URLs** (shorthand used in the tables)
- `ILCS:<code>` = `https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/<code>.htm` (for example `ILCS:040500050K3-550` = https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/040500050K3-550.htm)
- `IAC1470` = https://www.ilga.gov/agencies/JCAR/EntirePart?titlepart=06801470
- `IAC1375` = https://www.ilga.gov/agencies/JCAR/EntirePart?titlepart=06801375
- `IAC1283` = https://www.ilga.gov/agencies/JCAR/EntirePart?titlepart=06801283
- `IAC1400` = https://www.ilga.gov/agencies/JCAR/EntirePart?titlepart=06801400
- `PSYPACT` = https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=3902&ChapterID=10

**Versions to know about**
- 225 ILCS 20 (social work) and 225 ILCS 107 (counselors) both say "Section scheduled to be repealed on January 1, 2028" (sunset; expect them to be renewed).
- Pending amendments that change nothing relevant here: P.A. 104-742 (Telehealth Act and 356z.22, eff. 1-1-27), P.A. 104-465 (740 ILCS 110/4, eff. 7-1-27, only renames an agency), P.A. 104-598 (8-2001, eff. 1-1-27, wording only), P.A. 104-764 (225 ILCS 20/19 and 107/80, eff. 1-1-27; I quoted the text in effect now).
- Board rules: 1470.96 amended 48 Ill. Reg. 8825 (eff. 6-4-2024); 1375.225 amended 48 Ill. Reg. 8776 (eff. 6-4-2024); 1283.100 and 1283.125 eff. 8-9-2024; 1400.50 eff. 8-9-2024.

## Open questions / disputed

1. **3-550 and LMFTs.** 405 ILCS 5/3-550(c) names only the counselor Act, the social work Act and the psychologist Act. LMFTs are not named. It is unclear whether an LMFT may use the 12+ minor self-consent pathway. Treat it as unclear for LMFT and ask the clinician or an attorney to confirm.
2. **The 8-session cap applies under 17, not under 18.** The text says "under the age of 17", so a 17-year-old has no session cap, but parent-notice limits (a-2) still apply to all minors. The earlier prompt's "8 sessions of 90 min" is confirmed (P.A. 102-558, eff. 8-20-21). 3-501 was repealed by P.A. 100-614, and 3-5A-105 was renumbered by P.A. 102-558. 3-550 is the current section.
3. **740 ILCS 110/4(a)(3) wording.** The text says parent access is allowed if the teen "is informed and does not object **or** if the therapist does not find... compelling reasons". Read literally, either condition is enough. Many read it as the teen objecting **or** the therapist finding compelling reasons, either of which blocks access. This is unresolved. 3-550(a-2) separately allows denial "if the service provider finds that there are compelling reasons".
4. **602.11 compared with 110/4 for teens 12-17.** 602.11(b) says providers "shall grant access... to both parents". 110/4(a)(3) gives a teen's objection and the therapist's judgment weight, and 3-550(a-2) protects self-referred teens. 602.11(a) begins "Notwithstanding any other provision of law", but (b) does not. Treat mental health records of 12-17-year-olds as governed by MHDDCA/3-550 limits, and get legal confirmation.
5. **Who consents for a child under 12.** No Illinois statute names who must consent to outpatient psychotherapy for a child under 12. 410 ILCS 210/2 covers physicians, chiropractors, optometrists, APRNs, PAs and dentists only. The parent or guardian is the practical rule, inferred from 110/4(a)(1) and 602.5(b)(2) (health decisions include "psychological needs"). With separated parents, it is unclear whether one parent with decision-making authority is enough or both parents are needed. Ask for the allocation judgment.
6. **Married, pregnant or parenting minors.** 410 ILCS 210/1 makes their consent valid only for physicians, chiropractors, optometrists, APRNs and PAs, not therapists. Their own therapy consent rests on 3-550 (12+) or emancipation.
7. **The duty to warn is permissive in Illinois.** 740 ILCS 110/11(ii) and (viii) say records "may be disclosed". 405 ILCS 5/6-103 limits liability and sets how the duty is met, but by its terms it covers only "physician, clinical psychologist, advanced practice psychiatric nurse, or qualified examiner". LCSW, LCPC and LMFT are "qualified examiners" only if they meet 405 ILCS 5/1-122 (LCPC/LMFT need 3 years of post-master's experience). Whether other clinicians have a common-law duty is unsettled.
8. **FOID 24-hour reporting.** 430 ILCS 65/8.1(d) and 405 ILCS 5/6-103.3 bind physicians, clinical psychologists, APPNs and qualified examiners. Whether a non-qualified-examiner LCPC or LMFT must report is unclear (probably not, but best practice is to list it as a possible limit).
9. **740 ILCS 110/11(iv) collections scope.** The text ties disclosure to services "provided by a therapist or agency to a recipient under Chapter V of the Mental Health and Developmental Disabilities Code". It is unclear whether this covers private-practice billing at all. Using a collection agency may otherwise need written consent under 110/5. Get a legal check before any collections policy.
10. **Interest and late fees.** 815 ILCS 205/2 (5% without an agreement) and 205/4 (9% by written contract) are general rules with many exceptions (Section 4.05 and others). No Illinois rule specific to therapist late fees or no-show fees was found. Whether a flat late fee counts as "interest" was not researched. The Collection Agency Act was not reviewed (it covers agencies, not creditors collecting their own bills). Get an attorney's review before charging interest.
11. **Copy fees: 740 ILCS 110/4(b) "reasonable fee" compared with 735 ILCS 5/8-2001 caps.** Both seem to apply. The 8-2001 figures quoted are base amounts that are adjusted every year under 8-2006 (the current Comptroller figures were not pulled). For a patient's own access, federal HIPAA 45 CFR 164.524 (cost-based fee, 30 days) likely controls and may be lower. That is federal and was not researched here.
12. **Record retention.** Only the LCPC/LPC rule sets a period (7 years; for minors, 7 years after age 18). No Illinois retention period was found for LCSW, LMFT or psychologists. LMFT 85(a)(23) and SW 1470.96(a)(16) refer to "State laws", but none were found. Payer contracts and Medicare/Medicaid may require longer. Defaulting to 7 years (minors: age 25) is suggested.
13. **Telehealth consent.** Neither the Telehealth Act nor any of the four board rules requires a written telehealth consent. Illinois Medicaid (89 IAC 140.403) and payer contracts were not checked and may require one. The adopted national codes (ACA H.2, APA, NASW 1.03(e), AAMFT 6) expect telehealth-specific disclosure, so use the form as best practice.
14. **Compacts.** As of 2026-10-29, the ilga.gov chapter 45 and chapter 225 Act lists show PSYPACT (45 ILCS 195, eff. 1-1-20) and no Counseling Compact, Social Work Licensure Compact or MFT compact. The bill-status pages for pending 2026 bills were not checked.
15. **Sunset.** 225 ILCS 20 and 225 ILCS 107 are scheduled for repeal on 1-1-2028, and the Psychologist and MFT Acts on 1-1-2032. Recheck citations after renewal.
16. **Human Rights Act.** No client-facing notice or posting duty was found for small private offices (public accommodation). Employer duties apply with 1 or more employees. Federal ADA Title III and Section 1557 (if the practice takes Medicare/Medicaid or other federal money) were not researched.
17. **IDFPR website.** Board FAQs and guidance on idfpr.illinois.gov (telehealth for out-of-state clients, record retention) were not reviewed. Only statutes and JCAR rules were used.
